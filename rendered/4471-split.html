The three problems

parser — the shared parser reads every date month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first. A booking for 7 April (07/04) goes out as 4 July. This is what #4471 describes, and the fix queued for it.

hint — booking-v3 tells people to type the month first. The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field and then reads what people type the tenant’s way, day first. It only happens on the form channel of tenants who run booking-v3. These reports look the same as parser from the outside: day and month swapped. Fixing the parser does not touch the form, so they keep coming after #4471 closes.

move — the date moves by exactly one day. No day and month swap at all: “Tuesday 12 May” shown as “Monday 11 May”, a meeting held on Tuesday in Lisbon and Monday in Toronto, every June booking one day off. The desk already read these as a one-day move (desk-notes.json) but left them filed under parser, as a footnote. A month-first parser cannot move a date by one day; this looks like a time zone or daylight-saving shift. It needs its own ticket and its own owner.

Why intake tags do not help

Intake tags a report from the reporter’s browser the moment it arrives. The problem depends on the tenant: where they are, and which booking form they run (locales.ts). A tenant’s client on an en-US browser is still a day-first tenant. The table counts how many reports carry the wrong tag.

What changes

  • Each problem has its own ticket, with its own count. Close each one on its own fix.
  • When #4471 closes, watch for new date reports from booking-v3 forms and one-day moves: they are not fixed by the parser.
  • Merging on subject alone (decision 0001) put three problems into one ticket. Before you merge a report into a big ticket, check its cause with the dedupe explorer, and read the body when it does not say day and month swapped. Changing the rule needs a new decision record; 0001 stays as it is.
